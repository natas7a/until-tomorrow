What do we mean by "child safety" in an age of AI, and why is it so contested?

Before we start using the method, we need a shared picture of the field: the different issues it covers, how approaches differ between regions, and why people who all want to protect children still disagree. Framing: Whose rights, whose problem?

Core prep~65 min + exercise

This week's question

What exactly are we going to study, where, until when, and from whose perspective?

Framing turns a vast field into one clear focal issue that the cohort will carry through the rest of the fellowship. The deep dive frames "child safety" as a question of children's rights, to protection but also to provision and participation, and as a set of requirements for anyone building AI that children use.

Pre-session exercise

Week 3Step 02 · before Session 3

Scanning: Relationships with AI, and attributing feelings to machines

Core prep~57 min + exercise
Your progress0 done
This week's question

What is changing around our focal issue, how fast, and what deeper forces are behind it?

Children and teenagers are forming relationships with conversational AI: as a study aid, a confidant, a friend. Research suggests young children in particular tend to treat chatbots as if they were human. It's a fast-moving area, which makes it a good place to practise spotting signals of change.

⚠️ Content note

Some of this week's materials refer to serious harms, including self-harm, linked to chatbot conversations. Take care of yourself and skip anything you need to.

✏️ Exercise

Pre-session exercise

About 45 minutes. Find 3 signals of change related to our focal issue and add them to the Signal Wall in Session 3, activity 1:

  1. Phrase each one as a change, not a topic. "AI" ❌ → "Children increasingly turning to chatbots for emotional support" ✅

Core prep~75 min + exercise

This week's question

How do our signals connect, and where does the system reinforce or correct itself?

AI literacy is often put forward as a solution to children's safety online. But what does it mean, and who carries the responsibility? Countries are taking very different routes, from national curricula to handing students purpose-built chatbots. That gives us a rich system to map.

Week 5Step 04 · before Session 5

Scenarios: AI toys: a future still up for grabs

Core prep~53 min + exercise
Your progress0 done
This week's question

What plausible futures could unfold by 2040, and what would they feel like to live in?

Toys with built-in chatbots are already on sale, and major toy companies are partnering with AI developers. Very different futures are possible from here: trusted learning companions, a tightly regulated product category, a luxury item, or an unregulated mass market. As you read, look out for what could tip things one way or another.

⚠️ Content note

The PIRG report describes toys discussing sexually explicit topics and dangerous objects with children.

  1. Identify two critical uncertainties: factors that would strongly shape our focal issue by 2040, but whose direction we genuinely can't predict.
